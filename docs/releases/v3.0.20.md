# SWI3S Studio v3.0.20

A saved workspace now reopens the window exactly as it was, and names its captures relative
to itself. The Audio pane gains a per-stream Filter & Gain (DC blocker, zero-phase high-pass
and gain) and a spectrogram view chosen channel by channel, and data ports and config regions
can be named. A Visualizer CSV imported into a multi-region capture applies to one region,
large captures open in less than half the memory, and the fixed 8 GiB limit is gone.

Per-release detail is the changelog block at the top of `swi3s_studio/__init__.py`, newest
first; this file is the summary of the 3.0.20 entry.

## Workspaces

- **A saved workspace reopens the window as it was**: each Link's Commands filter and column
  widths and its Audio and Capture views (checked channels, zoom, Vertical Zoom, playback
  decimation, which channels are spectrograms and their frames, toggles), and for the window the pane layout (which panes are open, how they
  are docked and tabbed, which tab is in front, their sizes), its size and position, the
  timeline's zoom, the Commands sort and hidden columns, and the Samples, Registers,
  Statistics and Timing panes' settings. The playback device and bit depth stay with the
  computer.
- A workspace is now a `.swi3s` file (older `.json` ones still open). It records each capture
  relative to itself, so a folder holding both can be moved or shared, and asks for a capture
  it cannot find: locate it, skip that Link, or cancel.
- Switching Links, or a re-decode, no longer resets the Audio and Capture panes: each Link
  keeps its own view.

## Audio

- **Filter & Gain, per stream.** Right-click a stream in the Audio pane ▸ Filter & Gain…,
  or Audio ▸ Filter & Gain ▸ a stream, for a dialog laid out like Audacity's Amplify: a DC
  blocker (2nd-order Butterworth, 1 Hz corner, zero phase), a 4th-order zero-phase
  Butterworth high-pass (1 Hz to 20 kHz) and a gain. The gain opens at the value that
  normalises the stream after the filters, Allow clipping decides whether it may go past
  0 dB, and Preview shows the result before Apply. The waveform, playback and export use it;
  the Samples table keeps the decoded values; Revert, in the dialog, restores them exactly.
  Each Link keeps its own settings, through a re-decode and in a saved workspace.
- **Decode ▸ Block PDM DC Bias is gone**: the DC blocker replaces it, per stream and for PCM
  streams as well, and as a filter it also removes a bias that drifts. The old window-wide
  setting is not carried over, so a PDM stream opens with its bias until its DC blocker is
  ticked.
- **A spectrogram, channel by channel.** Right-click a channel ▸ Spectrogram draws it as a
  spectrogram, sharing the waveform's zoom, cursor and bookmarks, with the frequency under
  the pointer in the tooltip. Its frame size (256 to 2048 points) is chosen per channel: a
  click shows from half a frame before it, ±2.7 ms at 256 points and 48 kHz.
- **Data ports can be named.** Right-click a channel ▸ Rename…; the name is shown in the
  Audio, Samples and Capture panes, the Bus Grid's colour key (widening to fit), the Audio
  menus, the Filter & Gain and Export Audio dialogs and the Timing filter, and is saved with
  the Link.
- A port whose sample rate the decoder could not verify now has it measured from its
  samples, so it can be filtered, plays at its own pitch rather than at 48 kHz, and
  shows its rate.

## Decode

- **A Visualizer CSV imported into a capture with several config regions applies to the
  region under the cursor only.** It used to be imposed on the whole capture, so on a
  Safe-Lock-2 → 8 → 16 column capture every region was decoded at the CSV's width: every
  command turned CRC-red and the other regions lost their audio. Now that region decodes
  with the CSV's ports and width, everything else as the wire says; the timeline labels it
  `8col (CSV)`, and the workspace keeps it. Decode ▸ Remove … from This Region takes it
  out.
- **Opening a large capture takes less than half the memory**, so captures that asked for a
  time window now open whole. A 54 MB capture with 302M transitions peaked at 8.6 GB and
  was predicted at 14 GB, past the 8 GiB budget, however much RAM the machine had; it now
  peaks at 3.7 GB, is predicted at 7.2 GB, and opens.
- **No more fixed 8 GiB limit.** A capture is refused only when it is predicted not to fit
  in 60% of free memory; past 8 GiB the Open dialog says it is large and lets you open it.
  The decode that follows, whose size cannot be predicted, is watched instead: if free
  memory falls below 10% of RAM (at least 1 GiB) it stops and says so, and a stopped
  re-decode keeps the decode you had.
- **Config regions can be named**: right-click a region's band on the timeline ▸ Rename….
  The band reads `Playback · 8col`, and the name is saved with the Link.
- The native decode core changed (`score_abi` 12), so it must be rebuilt
  (`bash native/build_local.sh`, or `run.sh`/`run.ps1`, which rebuild a stale core).

## Examples

- The 18 directed-test configurations that report a bus clash, a placement error or a
  warning on purpose are in `visualizer_examples/directed_tests/intentional_errors/`, so the
  rest of the folder builds cleanly and the expected failures are easy to tell apart.

## Checks

- PySide6 is held below 6.12: with the first 6.12 release the process crashes as the
  interpreter exits, after every test has passed (seen on macOS and Linux). 6.11 is
  unaffected.
- The type check runs for macOS, Linux and Windows from any one machine, so code that runs
  only on another platform is checked too, and a new type error on any of them fails it.
- New tests check what the window shows against what its Links imply, across random
  sequences of Link operations and a save and reopen, and make each stage of loading a
  capture fail to check that the request still completes. A re-decode for a Link removed
  while it waited now reports as failed rather than as nothing.
