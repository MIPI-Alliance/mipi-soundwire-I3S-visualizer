# SWI3S Studio v3.3.0

The Timing Calculator draws any of its seventeen inequalities whole, every term on one time
axis with the margin last, and a maximized diagram steps through them with ← and →. Each row
ends in its margin and F_max, each column names its binding F_max, and Copy Summary, Export
Results and Export Diagram put the results on the clipboard, in a CSV file and in PNG, SVG or
PDF. Peripheral Placement sets every inequality, the P→M, P→P and contention inequalities
charge the length mismatch between the CLK and DATA traces, and the worst-corner search runs
off the UI thread, once per committed edit. The Bus Visualizer shows a data port by its name,
in a column as wide as the name, and a guard's Enable and Polarity are two settings. The
per-source CDS rows of a config CSV carry their register field names: SWI3S Studio 3.2.0 reads
a file saved by this version without its per-source CDS settings (below). With several Links
open, the dialogs and prompts name the Link they act on. Statistics has a Devices section,
regions with one column count are numbered, and a capture joined mid-stream decodes from its
first row.

## Changes

### Timing Calculator

- **A click on an inequality's heading or equation, off its terms, draws the whole
  inequality** in the diagram pane: every term on one time axis, the Specification column over
  the Example one, each term a span on one of two chains that close on the margin. A negative
  margin, and a term of the wrong sign such as a negative setup time, is a short span back. The
  signals are drawn at the pin the inequality is checked at, and at another pin only where a
  term is measured there, with the receiving device's GND and V_DD on each row. A crossing
  names its lane in its subscript (X<sub>MP,CLK</sub>, X<sub>PM,DATA</sub>), here and in the
  term diagram. Where two devices' flights cancel, the inequality is drawn at one pin, the
  other device's part moved there by its flight, and the footnote says so. A turn-on's data is
  dotted and labelled high-Z until its drive arrives, and a point after a release is drawn on
  the level the wire holds, here and in the term diagram. A label too wide for its span sits
  just past one of its ends or, where there is no room, nearby with a short leader whose
  arrowhead stops a clear gap short of its span; where short spans cluster, the arrowheads
  stand clear of each other and the leaders keep off the signal edges. Maximized, the two
  columns sit side by side, their time axes level.
  The page opens on inequality 1) drawn whole with its heading highlighted, and Reset and Load
  Example Settings return to it.
- **A maximized diagram steps through the inequalities.** **←** and **→** buttons beside the ⤢
  draw the previous or next inequality in numbered order, whole, and clear a highlighted term;
  the table follows, and restoring the diagram scrolls the inequality's whole block into view.
  The buttons show while the diagram is maximized and disable at the first and the last
  inequality. The **Left** and **Right** keys do the same while the focus is in the Timing page
  or nowhere; a list or tree elsewhere in the window keeps its arrow keys.
- **The diagrams colour the data by the device driving it**: the Manager's drive green, a
  Peripheral's (Peripheral A's) orange and Peripheral B's magenta, at every pin, in the term
  diagram and the whole inequality. A contention shows the releaser's drive, its release and
  the level the keeper holds in one colour and the acquirer's drive in the other. The undriven
  stretch before a turn-on is dotted in the colour of the device that last drove the wire, and
  each row's name takes its first edge's colour.
- **Each row ends in its margin and F_max.** Each column's substituted numbers end in
  "= <margin> ns (F<sub>MAX</sub> <rate> MHz)" (F_MAX in the copied summary): the margin green
  where it is positive and red where it is negative, the F_max red below the Clock Frequency
  Target, "(F<sub>MAX</sub> FAIL)" where the leg fails at the lowest rates, and absent where no
  rate bounds the leg or the margin does not depend on the clock rate: no UI term (UI, or N·UI
  with N > 0) at its corner and no Manager t_DD, t_ZD or t_DZ placed at a fraction of the UI by
  a 2× or 4× launch, as on MP_hold, PM_hold and PP_hold at the analog launch. The row's tooltip
  names the higher rates at which it passes again. The Margin and Max Frequency columns are
  removed. The inequalities pane fits its 90th-percentile row plus 15 %, so a much wider row
  scrolls sideways and the diagram takes the rest of the page.
- **Each column names its binding F_max** beside the *Timing Inequalities* title: the lowest
  F_max over the inequalities that bound the bus, each at its own worst corner, and the number
  of the inequality that sets it, red below the Clock Frequency Target, or "no limit" where no
  bounding inequality fails.
- **An inequality is named by its number and heading, a setup or hold inequality by its launch
  too.** The two inequalities that share a heading differ by the launch of the data:
  *1) Manager Setup for Peripheral, t<sub>DD</sub>* and *2) Manager Setup for Peripheral,
  t<sub>ZD</sub>*, in the whole inequality's title, the copied summary and Export Results
  (written t_DD and t_ZD). A heading over both launches and a contention or keeper inequality
  keep their titles.
- **Term names are shorter.** A crossing drops its ,late / ,early qualifier, since the term's
  sign gives the corner (`X_MP,rise,late` reads `X_MP,rise`), and keeps ,fast / ,slow; a
  SoundWire anchor correction or a ramp to V_OH / V_OL drops its ,fast (`Man_t_DD,anchor,rr`);
  the keeper's response reads `Man_t_Keeper`. This holds in the inequalities, the diagrams and
  the copied summary; settings and Example files are unchanged.
- **Copy Summary puts the results on the clipboard.** **File ▸ Copy Summary**, the page's
  right-click menu and **⌘C** (Ctrl+C) while the focus is in the page copy, as plain text, the
  margins and F_max of both columns per inequality, a binding F_max line per column, then each
  inequality's equation with its substituted numbers. A focused text or number box keeps ⌘C for
  its own selection.
- **Export Results… writes the inequalities and results as CSV**, from beside the page's buttons
  and from **File**: a title row, the inputs (spec pickers, ramp models, data launch, keeper,
  handover, placement, clock target, the SoundWire system size where a side is SoundWire, and
  the application version), the advisory line where it has text, then a row per inequality per
  column with its number, heading, column, equation, substituted numbers, margin, F_max as the
  page shows it and worst corner, in plain ASCII. The Example buttons read **Save Example
  Settings** and **Load Example Settings**, and the page's buttons sit at the right of the
  title's row. Save and Load Example Settings start in, and remember, the folder the Timing
  settings and Export Results dialogs use.
- **Export Diagram… writes the diagram as drawn**, the term diagram or the whole inequality, to
  PNG (2× by default, or 1×, 3× or 4×), SVG or PDF at the pane's width: from the diagram's
  right-click menu and from **File**, with **Export Diagram on Light Background…** in the dark
  appearance. SVG and PDF hold vector lines and text. The diagram's text takes its fonts'
  design widths on every platform, so each format places every label where the pane does.
- **The diagrams have their own drawing settings.** **View ▸ Diagram Colors, Lines and Fonts…**
  opens Preferences on a **Timing Diagrams** tab: each line class's colour per theme and weight,
  a style (solid, dashed, dotted or dash-dot) for the high-Z, rail, level, threshold and guide
  lines, the arrowheads, dots and ticks, the three drive colours, and each text's font family,
  size and weight. The open diagram redraws as a value changes, Cancel puts the previous
  settings back, and *Reset to Defaults* restores the tab. **Export Settings…** and **Import
  Settings…** write and read every setting as JSON, an unreadable entry reported as an import
  warning. By default a maximized diagram draws its waveforms, arrows, margin and axis 3 px wide
  and its levels, rails and guides 2 px wide, the V_DD and GND rails dotted, and its labels,
  headings, title and axis numbers at 15 px in regular weight. Beside the inequalities the
  diagram is drawn at 0.7 of that.
- **The diagram draws either column, or both, beside the inequalities too.** The column buttons
  beside the ⤢ show in the pane as well as maximized; one column alone takes the pane's whole
  width, the footnote names only that column, and Export Diagram writes what is drawn. The
  choice is kept across sessions, Both by default. The diagram's title wraps beside the buttons
  and the ⤢, or goes below them at the pane's full width where that takes fewer lines.
- **The top bar spans the page in fewer rows.** The Manager and Peripheral spec pickers sit one
  over the other, each with its ramp model beside it; the data launch shares a row with
  *Manager forces keeper*, Peripheral Placement shares one with *Handover UI*, and the clock
  packs beside them, onto further rows only where the window is narrow. The title's tooltip
  names the bus, the data launch selector's tooltip says what the launch mode costs at the
  current rate, and the Handover UI box's tooltip says what it allocates; the subtitle, the line
  under the data launch and the handover status line are removed. The advisory line takes no
  room while it is empty. The hint under *Timing Inequalities* reads *Click an inequality, or
  term, to highlight and draw it.*, its tooltip saying how to clear.
- **Peripheral Placement sets every inequality.** The picker (P→P placement in 3.2.0) offers
  *Worst Per Inequality* (the default), *All near the Manager*, *Driver near, sampler far*,
  *Driver far, sampler near* and *All at the far end*. *All near the Manager* puts every
  Peripheral at 0 cm, so t<sub>PD</sub> and t<sub>PD,mis</sub> read 0 on every Manager ↔
  Peripheral and contention inequality; *All at the far end* puts every Peripheral at the Bus
  Length's maximum. The two driver choices set the four Peripheral A to Peripheral B setup and
  hold inequalities only, each one layout with a Peripheral at each end, the far one at the Bus
  Length's maximum. Both columns follow the picker, and the Corner line names a pinned distance
  ("peripherals at 30 cm", "driver at 30 cm, sampler at 0 cm"). Saved settings and workspaces
  load their choice by the same key; one saved with *both near the Manager* or *both far from
  the Manager* now applies it to every inequality, not to the P→P inequalities alone.
- **The length mismatch between the CLK and DATA traces is charged where a clock and data
  meet at one device.** P→M setup and hold carry t<sub>PD,mis</sub> after the data's flight back
  to the Manager, a cost on setup and taken off the two flights' credit on hold; it is 0 under
  *All near the Manager* and where a hold inequality's worst corner puts the Peripheral at 0 cm.
  The four P→P setup and hold inequalities carry t<sub>PD,mis,AB</sub>, t<sub>PD,mis</sub>
  with the two Peripherals at opposite ends of the bus and 0 with both at one end. Each
  contention inequality carries −t<sub>PD,mis</sub> (P→P: −t<sub>PD,mis,AB</sub>) as its last
  term. Each term is shown under every placement, in the equation, the substituted numbers,
  the term diagram, the whole inequality and Export Results. The term diagram's note that the
  two flights net to zero names the trace mismatch as its own term, and is left out where the
  diagram draws no flight.
- **The term diagram draws each device at its own pin on P→M and P→P contention**, the acquirer
  at the Manager's end of the bus and the releaser a t_PD from it, the margin at the releaser's
  pin. It draws each edge the clock makes at a pin within a panel's time span: a launch whose
  panel reaches the next clock edge shows the clock falling there, and a handover two UIs long
  draws its clock as one row with the edge between. The whole inequality draws the clock edges
  a handover's UIs count through at every pin, a flight later at the far one.
- **EDE Delay is an Example-column range.** Each side's EDE Delay is a min / max pair on the
  Example column (3–7 ns by default), the contention inequalities taking its maximum and the
  keeper inequalities its minimum. The Specification column's EDE Delay cells read "does not
  apply", and its settings carry no EDE Delay; settings saved with one still load, without a
  warning. The EDE choice reads "SWI3S PHY2 EDE" in both spec selectors, and the column
  headings "PHY2 EDE" and "Example / PHY2 EDE". Saved settings name the choice by its key and
  load as before.
- **A SoundWire side follows the clock it is evaluated at.** A SoundWire Peripheral's t_DD /
  t_ZD minima and t_IS count from its earliest clock detection, V_IH,min, k/12 before
  SoundWire's 50 % point: its t_DD and t_ZD minimum is 9.9 ns and its t_IS −0.1 ns. A SoundWire
  side's t_IS and t_IH follow the Manager's clock t_RF: the setup and hold inequalities charge
  the row and its move from the slew it is derived at, shown as `Per_t_IS,ref,rise` or
  `Man_t_IH,ref,fall` (no term where the move is zero). A t_IS loses 1/12 (Peripheral) or 0.5
  (Manager) of the clock slew above 2.0 ns, and a Manager's t_IH gains 0.75 of the slew above
  t_Slew_Clock's max and loses it below. A Peripheral's t_IH does not move.
- **The page stays responsive while it computes.** The worst-corner search runs off the UI
  thread and evaluates each candidate corner's margins without building the term breakdown,
  which it builds once at each inequality's chosen corner; the results are the same. A typed
  number is computed when you commit it, with Enter or by leaving the box, not while you type or
  step it with the arrow keys, and a commit that leaves the value unchanged computes nothing. A
  selector, a checkbox, Reset and Load Example Settings compute at once. Focus leaving an
  Example row that was not edited changes no row, and an edit of one Manager launch row keeps
  the other rows' placement across a change of rate. While a search runs, every margin, F_max
  and substituted number reads "…", the diagram and the line beside the *Timing Inequalities*
  title say "Computing…", the binding F_max line is empty, and an edit made meanwhile starts a
  new search whose result alone is shown. The term diagram lays
  out faster; what it draws is unchanged.
- **The worst-corner search finds a τ, α or δ minimum between two samples of the range**, such
  as the kink where an edge's slew reaches the end of its range, to a tenth of a picosecond. No
  default page or spec pairing changes.

### Bus Visualizer and config files

- **A data port is shown by its name.** A named port's chip reads its name alone, and an
  unnamed one `DP<number>`; the number is in the chip's tooltip. A port's column widens so its
  chip shows the whole name, up to 200 px, a longer name shortened with "…" and whole in the
  tooltip; a loaded config or workspace opens with its columns sized the same way. A Name cell
  of `DP<slot>` or `DP<number>` in a config CSV reads as no name, so a renumbered, unnamed port
  keeps its chip after Save and Load Settings.
- **View Bus Grid in Visualizer carries the Analyzer's data port names over.** A port named in
  the Analyzer (Audio ▸ right-click a channel ▸ **Rename…**) opens in the Bus Visualizer under
  that name (the demos' `AMP1`, `AMP2`, `MIC1`, `MIC2`), in its chip and the maximized grid's
  key, and **Save Settings (CSV)** writes it in the Name row. **Export Visualizer CSV** writes
  the same Name row. A port with no name keeps its default.
- **A guard's Enable and Polarity are two independent settings.** The data port and per-source
  CDS guard controls are each an Enable checkbox beside a Polarity choice (0 / 1), as the FCP's
  are; the polarity stays editable, and is kept, with the guard off, and OK writes both while
  Cancel writes neither. The Visualizer, the Bus Analyzer and the native core read
  `CDS_GuardPolarity_REG_PerSource`, or a scalar `CDS_GuardPolarity_REG`, whatever the
  enables, and saving writes it back. A config CSV or workspace saved by an earlier version
  keeps its guards.
- **The per-source CDS rows of a config CSV carry their register field's name**, as the
  data-port rows do: `CDS_GuardEnabledPerSource` is `CDS_GuardEnable_REG_PerSource`,
  `CDS_GuardPolarityPerSource` is `CDS_GuardPolarity_REG_PerSource`, `CDS_TailWidthPerSource`
  is `CDS_TailWidth_REG_PerSource`, `CDS_DriveTypePerSource` is `CDS_DriveType_REG_PerSource`
  and `CDS_EndDriveEarlyPerSource` is `CDS_EndDriveEarly_REG_PerSource`. Every save, the
  bundled examples and the v1.74 converter write the new names. A file using the old names
  loads with the same settings; a row given under both names is a repeated row, and the later
  one is used. SWI3S Studio 3.2.0 and earlier do not read the new names: there, every CDS source
  of a file saved by this version takes its default (guard Off, tail width 0, drive type
  Normal, end drive early 0), and the import warnings name the rows not recognised or missing.
  A file that carries a per-source CDS row in neither spelling lists it among the missing rows
  that take their defaults, in the Visualizer and from the command line too.
- **Only the ManagerDataport flag makes a Manager data port.** In the Visualizer and from the
  command line, a port whose DeviceNumber_REG is outside 0..11 without the flag, −1 included,
  is disabled and reported as an import warning, as applying the CSV to a capture does, and a
  saved workspace config whose device number is outside −1..11 is too. Upgrading a v2.0 config
  writes such a port disabled.
- **The Visualizer's config reader reports more.** A field given on more than one row is
  reported, "<field> appears N times; the last row is used." (from the command line: "Line N
  (<field>) repeats line M; the later row is used."). Blank cells past the values a row needs
  are ignored, and a non-blank cell there is ignored and listed with its line; a row short of a
  value is still refused. A drawn port's DataPortNumber that is negative (the port keeps its
  column's number) or above 31 is noted, in the words the Bus Analyzer uses. A port's
  TailWidth, BitWidth, FCP_BitWidth or FCP_TailWidth of 3, or a PortMode of 1 (Reserved), is
  kept as written and named once per field, by the config CSV, workspace and Visualizer
  readers; PortMode 1 is drawn as Normal and raises no Test Mode notification beside a Normal
  port.
- **Upgrading a v2.0 config names the FCP registers it clears**: a port in FlowMode 0 or 1 has
  its six FCP registers written as 0, and each port whose file held a non-zero value is listed
  with the values cleared. Upgrading a v1.73 or v1.74 file names any row every legacy export
  writes that it lacks (Columns per Row, Row Rate [kHz], and the per-port Device Number,
  Channels, Sample Width, Interval, Offset, Horizontal Start, Horizontal Count, Source and Data
  Port Enabled rows); each takes its default.
- **The Visualizer's grid checks match the engine's placement.** A source port with both a guard
  and a tail that do not fit after its data reports "Tail would overflow row". Outside Sub-Row
  Interval mode, a port with Spacing above 0 whose pattern is shorter than its Horizontal
  Window has its tail and guard checked after its last channel group. A flow-controlled source
  port with a BitWidth above 0 is paired with its sink on the TxPresent bit's last UI, an
  unpaired one reported once per bit. A port whose SkippingNumerator_REG is at least its
  SkippingDenominator_REG is listed in the notifications and the headless report as *every
  interval is skipped*, with both values, as information, in place of an interval overflow
  error. With interval skipping on, a DRQ in an interval's last row and column is drawn, and
  checked by the decoder, only in transported intervals.
- **A click that opens a dialog leaves the window taking clicks.** A data port's name chip, its
  Device and Num Channels cells and a key swatch of the Analyzer's Bus Grid open their dialog
  when the click is released, so the table's checkboxes and buttons and the grid's ⤢ respond
  after the dialog closes. The Bus Visualizer's ⤢ restore gives the grid back the height it
  had, cycle after cycle.
- **A Manager data port has its own colour on the Analyzer's Bus Grid**, in place of the CDS
  grey, and a key swatch that opens its cell-label fields (labelled `Mgr·DP<n>` when unnamed
  beside other devices' ports). Its colour differs from Device 0's port of the same number in
  the grid's key, the Audio waveforms, the Capture overlay and the Samples port column.

### Multi-Link and the Bus Analyzer

- **The dialogs and prompts name the Link they act on.** With several Links open, Device Names,
  Register Maps, Hub Depths and Dataport Scramblers name in their title the Link they edit, and
  the device dialogs tick in an **On Bus** column each device that answers that Link's Pings.
  Manual SSP, Export Capture, Export Audio, Locate Sub-Capture (which says which Link it
  searched), Rename Data Port, Rename Region and Force Column Count name their Link. **Import
  Visualizer CSV** asks which Link the config is for, the Bus Grid's first, and imposes it on,
  or compares it against, that Link. In All Links, the Commands device filter reads *Devices
  (every Link)*.
- **A dialog or prompt acts on the Link it was opened on**, whichever Link is active when it
  closes: Audio ▸ Export, Dataport Scramblers, Devices, Force Column Count, a confirmed CSV
  import and Commands ▸ Export as CSV (the Link its proposed file name names).
- **With several Links open, the Bus Grid draws its own Link's instant** when another Link is
  active and the Links are offset in time, and **Decode ▸ Remove … from This Region** names and
  removes the CSV of the Bus Grid's Link.
- **All Links reaches every Link.** Next / Previous SSCR/DSCR Commit step through every Link's
  commits in shared time, making each commit's Link the active one. A Commands filter reaches
  every Link's timeline band, which draws, and steps with ← / → through, its own Link's commands
  the filter leaves. A Link added while Capture and Audio show All Links gets a lane that draws
  it, and an Audio lane for a further Link plays on the chosen output device and bit depth.
  Turning All Links off, or removing a Link, keeps each lane's Capture and Audio view with its
  Link.
- **Statistics has a Devices section**, between Commands and Peripheral Reports: one fold per
  device that answers Pings, is addressed by a command or carries a data port, headed by its
  command count and name, holding its Ping responses and alerts, the commands addressed to it by
  kind, bytes read and written, errors by kind, reply-CRC failures, reported faults, SSPs and
  data ports, then the Manager's data ports.
- **Statistics ▸ Link Control measures Man_tPhyStart.** A Cold or Warm Start lists the span from
  the PhyStart falling edge to the first edge of audio mode, flagged against 64–96 µs; a ring at
  that falling edge, or a glitch pair on either line, is not taken as that first edge. It is not
  listed when the capture ends before that edge, nor for a PHY3 (DLV) bring-up.
- **Regions with the same column count are numbered**, from 1 across the capture (`16-col 1`,
  `16-col 2`), in the timeline bands, the Timing pane's region selector, Statistics and the
  Decode dialogs; a region the user named shows that name instead. Statistics counts its regions
  from 1. A workspace keeps the Timing pane's region by its position, so renaming the region
  keeps it; one saved by an earlier version, which holds the region's label, finds the region
  by that label or by the label's time span.
- **The Timing pane and Statistics follow the playhead's region.** Moving the playhead into
  another region, or raising the tab, selects that region, at the top of Statistics (or its
  Regions title when the section is folded). The Timing pane's region selector is as wide as
  its longest name.
- **The Timing pane zooms horizontally.** Scrolling over the setup or the hold histogram zooms
  that side alone, 0 at the join, re-binned over the span shown, down to four sample periods or
  1/1000 of that side's span, whichever is larger; Reset Zoom shows both sides whole.
- **Play returns to where it started.** At the end of the clip, and on Stop, the playhead goes
  back to where Play started, so the next Play plays the same passage; Stop with the playhead
  already there takes it to the start of the audio. A Play that resumes a Pause keeps the first
  Play's start.
- **The panes stay usable while a re-decode runs.** While *Decoding capture…* is up after a
  scrambler, hub-depth, SSP, register or config CSV change, panning, zooming and moving the
  cursor show the decode from before the change, whole, and every pane switches to the new
  decode at once when it is ready. A data-port or region name, or a register map, set meanwhile
  is kept. The re-decode's bar shows its progress, and closing the window cancels a re-decode,
  a workspace load or a demo load within moments.
- **A capture whose decoded audio exceeds 1 GiB keeps its audio and command table on disk**, in
  a temp directory removed when the capture is closed or replaced, its Link removed, a re-decode
  replaces its results or the window closes; a smaller capture keeps them in memory. Its
  re-decode writes its results apart from the ones on screen, so a re-decode that fails or is
  cancelled leaves them as they were. The Register Map at the cursor continues from a register
  checkpoint rather than replaying every command before it.
- **Compare diffs the expected config against the registers at the playhead.** The box counts
  the registers that differ from the values the Registers pane shows at the playhead, and says
  so; when a playhead move changes the count, the status bar gives the new one.
- **Audio of unknown sample rate is played and exported at 48 kHz, and says so.** **Audio ▸
  Export…** names each such stream in its completion box, and Play names them in the status
  bar. The completion box lists under *Resampled* only the streams written at another rate.
- **The demos' PCM tones are dithered**: each sample of 2 bits or more carries TPDF dither of up
  to 1 LSB before it is rounded, a fixed function of the stream and the sample number, so a
  demo is the same on every run. The demos place every SSP on a whole pattern period.
- **A development build names its commit.** Run from a source checkout past a release, the
  window title, About and the Timing results export read like `3.3.0+12 (1a2b3c4)`: the
  release, the commits since its tag and the commit, with `, modified` while tracked files
  differ from it. A checkout at the release tag shows the release number alone, or with
  ` (<commit>, modified)` while tracked files differ; an installed release, or a checkout
  without git or the release tag, shows the release number alone.

### Decoding

- **A sync-point command with a Row_Delay outside 14..15 has no effect anywhere.** A CRC-valid
  SSPA, or confirmed SSCR or DSCR, whose Row_Delay is not 14 or 15 names no SSP row. The
  Commands table flags it "Programming error: Row_Delay N is outside 14..15; the command had no
  effect", and the decode, the Bus Grid, the Registers pane, the Commit Points, a partial
  capture's phasing and a register's History give it no effect. The timeline draws such a
  commit with the short Commit tick, hovering as *Commit*, and such an SSPA as a plain command,
  and Statistics leaves it out of the SSP counts.
- **An SSP part-way through a running port's interval is an unexpected SSP.** The SSCR or SSPA
  that generated it is flagged "SSP mid interval (row in interval != 0)", as the peripheral
  asserts IntStat_UnexpectedSSP there. An SSP part-way through a skip pattern is flagged also
  where the command reconfigures the ports.
- **A capture joined mid-stream decodes from its first row.** With a config CSV and an SSPA (or
  a manual SSP) in the capture, every port, a skipping one included, takes the phase the Stream
  Sync Point sets from the first row. A PHY3 (DLV) capture's recovered clock locks once 64
  consecutive rows open on a rising edge and works back to the first row in the capture,
  wherever in a row it starts and with the analyzer's clock off nominal, and a PHY3 capture
  brought up again later decodes both Links. A sample already in flight when the capture starts
  is not shown, nor are a scrambled stream's first samples, which its descrambler recovers from
  bits sent before the capture; the import warnings name those streams.
- **A bring-up detected in a recording joined mid-stream is dropped** when it never reaches
  audio and a CRC-valid command decodes inside its phases. A Warm Start there is dropped when
  the Link was in audio mode up to it with no clock stop between: a CRC-valid command before its
  pulse, or more than 16 clock edges between the last clock gap of 1 ms or more and the pulse.
- **A decode that loses its commands holds the column count.** After 8192 rows with no CRC-valid
  command the decoder re-hunts the comma at the column count it has, keeping the audio in step.
  Only when a second such stretch follows does it detect the column count afresh; a different
  count adopted that way opens a region and raises an import warning naming both counts and the
  sample.
- **A SkippingDenominator write reaches every running port.** `SLC_SkippingDenominator` is
  single-ranked, so a write to it, or a commit that changes it or the column count, applies to
  every running port from the row it takes effect, a port whose own registers did not change
  included: its audio and bit-sample markers skip the intervals the new ratio names, as the grid
  does. A write that starts on a read-only byte and covers PortControl, FlowMode or another
  single-ranked port register reconfigures the decode at once, as one that starts on it does.

### Other changes and fixes

Timing Calculator:

- Timing settings that leave a value out load the selected pair's default for it: opening a
  settings file or workspace sets the Manager and Peripheral specs first, seeds F_CLK, both ramp
  models and every row for that pair, and applies the file's values over them. A value that is
  not a number, or a ramp model that is not a choice, is reported and takes the default. A file
  naming a clocked launch mode and omitting the Manager launch rows seeds them as choosing that
  mode by hand does.
- The Specification column always holds the selected pair's tables: a saved number that differs
  from this version's tables is listed in the Import warnings window ("Specification value(s)
  differ from this version's tables, which are used").
- An older settings file's `phy` that is not the integer 1 or 2 (`true` included) gives SWI3S
  PHY2 and, unless the file names both specs, is reported.
- File ▸ Open Timing Settings reads a file saved with a byte-order mark. A settings file over
  1 MiB, or nested too deep to read, is refused with a *Couldn't read that file* warning, as
  Load Example Settings refuses it. Load Example Settings names a row whose entry holds neither
  `min` nor `max`, and a key other than `min`, `max` and `pick`.
- "No margins" quotes the value a row holds: every row is checked at its minimum and at its
  maximum before the corner search, so the notice names the typed end the model refuses (τ at
  10000 %), and a τ of exactly 100 % is named.
- F_max stops at the toggle ceiling of the slowest edge the slew rows allow on every leg,
  whichever slews the leg's worst corner picks.

Bus Visualizer, config files and register maps:

- A config CSV with no DeviceNumber_REG row decodes the ports the row does not cover on Device 0,
  as Load config and the Bus Visualizer place them; a missing row is reported.
- A config CSV port whose HorizontalCount_REG is near the largest int places its transports
  from HorizontalStart_REG on, in the audio and the grid, and a NumColumns_REG near it decodes
  at 32 columns on every platform.
- A legacy (v1.73/v1.74) Visualizer config CSV is recognised as a config: Decode ▸ Import
  Visualizer CSV upgrades and reads it, and Open Capture refuses it with the hint to open it
  with File ▸ Open Visualizer CSV. Replacing, removing or reopening a Link whose config CSV was
  upgraded from an older format, and each `api.decode_capture` call, leaves no
  `swi3s_cfg_*.cur.csv` in the temporary folder.
- The decoded Bus Grid skips a port's Tail that falls in a Sync0 column, the Manager driving
  that UI, as the Bus Visualizer draws it.
- Register-map field defaults: a field written `<DefaultValue/>` takes its register's default;
  a field's `X` bits take the register default's bits, and its known bits win, so a byte piece
  gets a reset value wherever the two together know every bit (`0xXX01` across bytes resets its
  byte 0 piece to 1); an unwritten register byte resets to its fields' known bits over the
  register's default. A field default that disagrees with its register's default is reported,
  naming the register, the field, both defaults and the field's bits; bits either leaves
  unknown are not compared.

Bus Analyzer:

- Re-decodes queued behind a running decode all take effect: one replaces a queued one only
  when both set the same thing (the SSP, the scrambler overrides, the hub depths, one register's
  what-if, one region's column pin or imported CSV). ⌘L / ⌘K pressed before the previous step's
  re-decode lands steps from the row that step sets. A re-decode that finishes after you moved
  to another mode leaves you there. An open drops the re-decodes queued for the Links it
  replaces, and replaces a Link still being added, whatever its decode's progress.
- A re-decode keeps the Commands column widths. A Commands column hidden by default is fitted
  to its header as well as its contents when first shown. The playhead's command and sample are
  the top rows of the Commands and Samples tables.
- Next / Previous SSCR/DSCR Commit leave the playhead where it is when there is no commit beyond
  it.
- The timeline's hover names only commands whose ticks are drawn.
- Returning to the window keeps the active Link: the focus a pane gets back as the window is
  activated, or as a menu closes, does not change it; a click in a pane, or Tab into it, does.
- Export Capture asks before replacing a file: one question names the files the export writes
  that already exist and were not confirmed in the save dialog, a .bin export's
  `<name>-digital-<channel>.bin` files included, default No. A name typed in the File field
  gets the format's extension (`run7` → `run7.sal`), and changing Save as replaces only a
  `.sal`, `.bin` or `.csv` ending (`run 1.5` → `run 1.5.csv`).
- Audio ▸ Export with *Visible range (audio view)* refuses a ticked stream with no audio in that
  range, naming it. 96 kHz and 192 kHz streams resample to 44.1 kHz at the exact rate.
- Filter & Gain: a saved high-pass on a stream whose rate is unknown is skipped and its gain
  applied; a setting whose only change is a ticked Allow clipping is kept in the dialog, a saved
  workspace and a re-decode, leaving the samples as decoded; Apply, Preview or Enter pressed
  before the peak is remeasured measures it first, so the clipping guard holds. A workspace's
  Filter & Gain row whose gain or corner is text, true/false or not finite, or with an on/off
  flag that is not true, false, 0 or 1, is not read: the stream opens unprocessed and the row is counted
  in the import warning.
- Statistics folds a deferred read only when its ReadData delivers data; a ReadData that failed
  or got no answer stays a record of its own. Statistics counts only the SSPs the bus acts on,
  leaving out a sync-point command that failed its CRC, an unconfirmed SSCR and one whose
  Row_Delay the decode rejected. A data port whose sample size changes shows its size range and
  no bandwidth. Statistics ▸ Data Ports names a Manager data port `Manager DP<n>`. Statistics'
  pass and fail values take a new theme's colours at once.
- The Timing pane assigns a data edge on a clock edge's own sample to the next edge, as the
  decoder reads it. A Timing region ends where the Link stopped before a later bring-up, and a
  data edge whose clock gap is longer than 1.5 times the phase's median gap (a clock pause) is
  not measured.
- Locate Sub-Capture marks at most 64 matches and, when the capture holds more or the search
  stopped collecting, gives the number found ("at least" for the latter) and the number marked.
  Locate refuses a .wfm reference whose two files differ in record length, sample rate or start
  time, and does not crash when its search ends during a progress update.
- The Audio channel labels stay legible in the light theme: a label whose trace colour reads
  under 3.2:1 on the channel list is drawn in a shade of it, in the same hue, and the waveform
  keeps the colour itself. The Samples pane's Port column and the Cursors table's pair rows take
  the same shade.
- Commands ▸ Export as CSV names a vendor register from the addressed device's imported register
  map, and an address outside every map by its address region; the All Links export uses each
  Link's own maps. A register or Link name starting with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` is written with a
  leading quote.
- The symbol pane holds a phase's framing through its Manager Packet and ReadData, so a K.28.7
  pattern across a symbol boundary there is not shown as a comma; with a hub depth set, the
  idle symbols between phases show. A deferred read's delivery is labelled with its own
  device's byte count.
- A later FBCSE bring-up opens a new bus row: when the Link stops mid-row, that row keeps its
  number and the restart's first row takes the next. A clock pause starts a fresh clock-rate
  measurement. A column count pinned on the region a clock pause opens outranks a config CSV.
- A change to a register's `_CURR` value (a commit, a bus read of the `_CURR` alias, *Force
  value…*) changes the `_CURR` row of the Register Map only; the `_NEXT` row keeps the value
  written there.
- The Registers device choice is kept while the cursor is before the device's first write (its
  defaults shown), and a workspace reopens with it whatever the activity at the restored cursor;
  a device number outside 0..11 is named in the import notes.
- A name with an ampersand (`R&D`) reads as typed in View ▸ Links, Audio ▸ Playback Decimation,
  Audio ▸ Filter & Gain, Decode ▸ Remove … from This Region, the Timing filter and the Audio
  channel list.
- Export Capture, Export Audio, Devices, Import Visualizer CSV, Import Peripheral Register Map,
  Filter & Gain and the Filter Expression dialog draw in the window's theme, the Filter
  Expression dialog changing with View ▸ Appearance while open. The recovered bit clock trace
  follows the theme.
- Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts lists the timeline's ← / → and ⌘← / ⌘→ under *Timeline (when
  focused)*. The status bar names a mode as the Mode menu does.
- Removing a pair's first cursor and placing another completes the pair. A Bus Grid persistence
  scan that lands late leaves the grid's "No PHY Selected" or "Link Inactive" message, and the
  scan runs on a plain worker thread. A Capture or Audio click seeks only from the data area,
  to a sample inside the capture. A Capture lane with no sampled bits in the window clears its
  channel labels. Scrolling back in the CDS table keeps the selected symbol selected. A
  spectrogram lane is analysed once per burst of zoom, pan or resize steps. Opening a capture
  over several Links while Commands shows All Links leaves the table header claiming no sort.
- An analog threshold typed as text is refused: "give each as a number of volts".

Capture import:

- A windowed `.sal` open decodes and checks each block after the window, one at a time, so the
  open takes about as long as reading the whole file. A block that does not decode is an import
  warning naming its channel and where the block starts, and a channel whose compressed data or
  checksum is damaged past the window one naming the sample it can be read to; the window
  opens. A window that runs past that sample is refused.
- A binary capture with more than 64 KiB ahead of its transition blocks opens. A `.sal` whose
  meta.json lists no usable digital channel takes the channel numbers from the `digital-N.bin`
  names, with a warning, and a version-0 channel holding more transitions than its header counts
  is read to the count, with a warning. A file opened as several Links shows each import warning
  once.
- A digital CSV or `.bin` with an idle lead-in keeps its sample rate; where a few off-grid
  timestamps make a finer grid the only one that holds them all, a digital CSV opens at that rate
  with a warning naming the coarser grid. A digital CSV's transition times are rounded to the
  sample grid, and times more than a quarter sample off it, among each channel's first 65,536
  transitions, are reported per channel.
- Lone-CR line ends: a digital CSV is counted as rows for the memory guard, and a scope analog
  CSV is recognised and priced by its rows. The CSV line limit counts a line's characters, not
  its line ending.
- Open Capture prices each Link of a `.sal` by its own two channels, a scope CSV's cost includes
  sorting rows out of time order, and a `.wfm` cut short is refused as truncated, naming the
  bytes it holds.

Workspaces:

- A reopened workspace decodes with its saved hub depths and scrambler overrides, restores the
  Commands filter against the Commands pane's Link, keeps an Audio selection with every channel
  unchecked, keeps a Manager data port's Evenly Spaced stream, and keeps a cursor placed on a

- A lost relative capture is looked for beside the workspace alone, and the Locate prompt names

  and once one file of a pair is located the other is taken from the same folder; on macOS and

Command line:

### Packaging and the Python API

- **The native decode core changed (`score_abi` 19)**, so it must be rebuilt
  (`bash native/build_local.sh`). VCD import reads the value changes in the native core, with
  the same edges, levels, import warnings and errors, and a `.sal` export encodes each channel's
  transitions in bulk, writing the same bytes.
- **The desktop bundle ships the third-party notices of the components inside the Qt libraries**
  (`THIRD-PARTY.txt`), and those of the interpreter's OpenSSL and ncurses libraries where the
  bundle holds them. Its build refuses an environment holding installed packages beyond the
  runtime requirements and the build tools, naming each one (`docs/PACKAGING.md`, section 3).
- **The bundle holds only the Qt modules the app loads**: QtVirtualKeyboard, QtQuick, QtQml and
  QtPdf, the virtual-keyboard and PDF plugins that bring them, and the TUIO touch plugin are
  left out. The Qt for Python and FFmpeg notices say where `LICENSE.md` is in the bundle.
  `tools/bundle_constraints.txt` pins the PySide6 release the notice was reviewed for, and the
  build stops on a Qt for Python wheel of a release series, or with a licence statement, the
  notice does not name.
- `swi3score.TransitionSampleSource` and `DlvSampleSource` accept a signed integer edge array,
  NumPy's default int64 included, when no edge is negative (a C-contiguous int64 array without a
  copy); a negative edge raises ValueError and a floating-point array TypeError.
- `decode()` notes a forced column count outside 2..32 (the count is then detected) and a
  scrambler override whose key is not a (device, data port) pair (left out), as
  `decode_capture` does, and `decode_capture(forced_column_count=N)` decodes at N on a capture
  whose bring-up recovered a Safe-Lock width or with a config CSV.
- The timing calculator refuses a fractional `handover_UIs`, naming it. `spec_source.preset()`
  gives the proposed PHY2 and EDE sources a t_DZ of 0–9 ns on both sides, the values the page
  seeds.

## Testing

- **CI runs the whole suite once**: the core job runs every test but perf on ubuntu 3.14 under
  coverage, measured through `sys.monitoring` (`COVERAGE_CORE=sysmon`), with the 82 % floor.
  Ubuntu 3.11/3.12/3.13, Windows 3.12/3.14 and macOS 3.12 run the platform scope,
  `(gui or platform or smoke) and not perf`, the expression the guest gate runs, with a
  150-minute limit; `smoke` marks the core smoke set (`CORE_SMOKE` in `tests/conftest.py`). CI
  runs on a push to main or a `vX.Y` branch, on every pull request and by hand through
  `workflow_dispatch`.
- **A test that builds widgets is marked `gui`**, by its file or by itself; the calculator-only
  tests of `test_timing_cross_spec.py` and `test_timing.py` are `core`. The Timing page's checks
  over every spec pairing run at the calculator (`test_timing_view_columns.py`), from the inputs
  the page's seed tables give (`tests/timing_page_inputs.py`), with one pass of each through the
  page in the platform scope and one test pinning those inputs against the page.
- **The Timing test modules share the calculator's results within a module** (`shared_compute`
  in `tests/conftest.py`); a non-empty `SWI3S_SHARED_COMPUTE_AUDIT` evaluates every read again
  and fails where the two differ.
- **The term diagram's layout sweeps are split by scope.** The guests and CI's platform jobs
  sweep it at stacked 380 px for every input set but the default, which they sweep at 380, 900
  and 1580 px, plus the cases `_COVER` lists; the rest run in the whole suite. The release
  gate's `diagram-cover` check (`tools/diagram_cover.py`) fails when the cases left to the whole
  suite reach a branch of the term diagram or the whole-inequality drawing the platform scope
  does not. The screenshots check renders the Filter Expression dialog in light and dark,
  started in a theme and switched live.
- **`tools/changed_tests.py`** follows the package's own imports, so a module reached only
  through other package modules runs their tests; `--run` takes `-n N` (`--workers N`),
  defaulting to `$SWI3S_TEST_WORKERS` when it is a whole number (0 read as 1) and else to one
  per CPU (an option count that is not a whole number of at least 1 is refused with exit 2),
  and exits 3 for a changed package or native file that no test file covers, whatever the
  tests it ran did.
- **`tools/publish_tree.py`'s check 3b finds partial, short-line, binary and mode reverts**: a
  deleted run of a contribution's added lines, a reverted version pin, a binary or empty file
  the contribution added or changed that is absent, or a changed one whose content is its
  parent's again, and an executable-bit change reverted (not checked on Windows). It prints
  the upstream commit and date it compared against.
- **New coverage**: the VCD reference suite runs on the native reader and on the NumPy fallback
  and feeds the native reader cases cut in two at every byte, and `test_perf.py` times a
  10M-change VCD; a transport checkpoint carries every port's channel and FCP descrambler state,
  which `Decoder.checkpoint_descramblers()` reads back and a test compares with an independent
  descrambler; PHY3 join-late cuts at four transition counts and six points in a row, with the
  analyzer's clock 3 % off nominal and followed by a Cold Start, match the full decode from the
  cut; and tests pin the Row_Delay rule, TxPresent pairing, an immediate SkippingDenominator
  write, the guard's enable and polarity in all three loaders and the three guard dialogs, the
  data port names carried to the Visualizer and its CSV, the maximized grid's colour key, the
  Timing Diagrams settings, the Timing settings restore rules, and the workspace, All Links and
  Commands table fixes above.
